# Terra implementation checklist for the public fixer-upper site

**The first acceptance test starts at the homepage in a signed-out browser.** A buyer can find a Bay Area home, understand what work it may need, tap **Connect with an agent**, see that Roozbeh will receive the request, and submit it. Roozbeh can sign in and see which home and question prompted that inquiry. A second visitor can use **Request a tour**. A third can apply to become a partner agent. Build all three journeys in the first reviewable version.

The product is free for buyers. Someone deciding whether they could afford to live in a home should find its asking price, condition, and renovation budget before encountering an investor profit calculation. The interaction to borrow from Zillow is the ability to contact a buyer’s agent or request a tour directly from the home being viewed. Use Flip Finder’s own branding, agent identity, and routing. [Zillow’s buyer tour flow](https://www.zillow.com/buy/touring/)

## First delivery — the working buyer site

### 1. Publish only the listing data a buyer may receive

- [ ] Implement a publisher with explicit permissions per originating source and per listing. Map the actual feed fields for internet display, address, geolocation, media, public remarks, valuations, comments, sold comps, AI processing and derived output. Record the approved basis, effective time and refresh deadline. Unknown permissions leave the affected field or feature disabled. An otherwise approved listing can still appear with limited features.
- [ ] Construct a published record field by field. Candidate display fields include an opaque public ID, permitted location label, asking price, status, property type, beds, baths, square footage, approved public remarks and media, required credits and update time. Include coordinates and analysis only where allowed. Do the same for each comp. Publish only approved agent/brokerage credit fields from business cards. Keep owner and occupant details, showing instructions, lockbox information, private remarks, agent-only contacts, internal notes, review identities, credentials and raw provider objects out of the public record.
- [ ] Give the public reader access only to published inventory. Keep raw-source credentials with the publisher/private service. Give public form handlers narrow write access for submissions. Use the same published representation for cards, detail, gallery, map, metadata, previews, and any API or sitemap. Check narrative text, photo URLs and captions for suppressed information too. A private comp can affect both narrative and computed output. Invalidate dependent output and recompute from permitted evidence before serving it again.
- [ ] On withdrawal, unpublish the detail, remove the card, map pin and search counts, reject new property requests, and expire cached content and controlled media. On address suppression, remove street text, identifiable links, precise coordinates and other location clues. Keep the home in the list where permitted. On valuation suppression remove the estimate, profit, ceiling, calculator inputs and any text or filter that discloses them. Process permission changes before queued analysis and recheck eligibility before saving or publishing its output.
- [ ] Make the complete anonymous experience reviewable immediately with clearly labeled synthetic listings and cleared images in a preview dataset. Include a complete home, a home awaiting analysis, a home with a suppressed address and a home with limited media. Keep test inquiries in the preview inbox. Real inventory is enabled source by source after Roozbeh supplies the broker and MLS decisions from the original report.

**Pass when** a fixture with distinctive private-field values and an unexpected new MLS column has none of those keys or values in any public response, including page source and map data. Repeat after warming the detail cache and withdrawing the listing. A signed-out user and a demo user both lack access to the private lead inbox.

### 2. Turn the homepage and search into the buyer entrance

- [ ] At `/`, put “Find a Bay Area fixer-upper” above a labeled city selector and **Browse homes** button that submits to `/homes`. Keep “All Bay Area cities” as a working default. Provide visible **Find homes** and **For agents** navigation. Make the existing explanation secondary. Replace illustrative price or profit examples in the main shopping area with published homes or clearly labeled preview listings.
- [ ] At `/homes`, query *all eligible active published homes*, including ones missing an assessment, photos or coordinates. Populate city choices and counts from the same inventory. Support city, price range and minimum beds with shareable query parameters. Add a clear-filters action, stable pagination, and a useful empty state. Preserve filters and page when returning from a detail.
- [ ] On desktop, show a list beside a map. On mobile, default to the list with a **Map** toggle. Each card leads with a photo or accessible fallback, asking price, permitted location, beds/baths/size, and a simple **View home** action. Show a reviewed renovation range or an accurate availability label when allowed. A missing range never becomes zero. Use public detail URLs for cards and map popups. Keep map-free listings browsable and the list usable when tiles fail.
- [ ] Add straightforward discovery filters where the feed supports them. Offer **45+ days**, **60+ days**, **Price reduced**, and explicit **Auction** or **Bank-owned** flags. Hide unsupported filters. Use documented dates, prices and source designations. Trust ownership and days on market alone cannot identify a distressed seller.

**Pass when** a signed-out search returns both an assessed and an unassessed eligible home. The card and pin open the same public detail. Change a filter, open a home, then go back on mobile and verify the prior results.

### 3. Make the agent reachable from the home

- [ ] At `/homes/<public_id>`, order the first screen around a lead photo/gallery, asking price, permitted location, basic facts and the agent action. Put **Connect with an agent** in a prominent desktop contact card beside the listing summary with **Request a tour** directly below. On mobile put both actions in a persistent bottom bar with safe-area spacing and enough bottom padding to reach the last content. Keep both available on listings with pending analysis or missing photos.
- [ ] In the contact card write “Ask Roozbeh about this home.” Show Roozbeh’s verified display name, role as the buyer’s contact, responsible brokerage, license details and approved contact disclosures from configuration. Use a supplied authorized portrait if available. Show “Listed by” with the originating listing agent, brokerage, source and update time in a separate attribution area. Use clearly marked sample identity details when verifying fixtures.
- [ ] Below the summary, organize the page into **Renovation scope**, **Comparable sales**, and **Purchase scenario** when permitted. Give each section a plain-language takeaway and an expandable evidence view. Add an **Ask about this estimate** action that opens the same contact form with the appropriate question preselected. Keep losses visible where a supported scenario produces one. Name the break-even price accurately.
- [ ] Provide direct GET/POST routes at `/homes/<public_id>/contact` and `/homes/<public_id>/request-tour`. A drawer on desktop or sheet on mobile may enhance these pages. The links and forms must work by direct URL, keyboard, and with JavaScript disabled. Keep focus visible, label inputs, restore focus when closing a sheet, and show errors next to the affected fields.

**Pass when** at 390px and 1440px wide a buyer can see the price and reach an agent from the listing without scrolling through the analysis. The form identifies the home and recipient before asking for contact information. Neither action goes to login, a generic contact page, or a mail app.

## Analysis delivery — implement alongside the working buyer site

### 5. Give renovation ranges a scope and a source

- [ ] Extend the assessment schema and reviewer workflow with versioned cost items. Capture room, work description, quantity, unit, low/high unit price, source type, source/date/geography, evidence photo IDs, confidence, inclusion and alternative group. Separate observed condition, seller-reported issues, and inspection questions. Let reviewers attach a local bid or invoice with public-safe provenance. Roozbeh obtains contractor pricing. Terra implements the input and display paths.
- [ ] Compute line extensions, room subtotals and project totals in code. Sum only selected items, apply quantity once, and allow one choice per mutually exclusive scope. Expand a compact room row by tap or keyboard to show the basis. Mark a partial budget explicitly. Keep legacy observations readable. Publish a numeric room total only when it has a validated scope and calculation.
- [ ] Use 2988 Orchid, Fairfield, as a private regression case. The saved **$2k–$4k vanity** is a component. The saved **$8k–$16k for two cosmetic baths combined** is a model estimate. Aida’s **$25k–$40k per full bath** implies **$50k–$80k for two** for a different scope. Show provenance and alternatives. Flag the seller-reported roof for a roofer’s quote. Keep a cabinet allowance attached to cabinetry. Use a synthetic equivalent to demonstrate this publicly until the actual data is cleared.
- [ ] Reconcile the buyer scenario against a versioned ledger covering purchase, financing, renovation, hold and resale. Make tax, insurance, HOA, utilities, contingency, staging, marketing, photography, notary, attorney, title/escrow and transfer costs visible where applicable. Show which are included in bundled fees and which remain unknown. A buyer can distinguish the renovation budget, estimated result at asking, break-even purchase price, and an offer meeting a chosen profit target.

**Pass when** switching the two-bath scope replaces the prior allowance and recalculates the total exactly once. Under the original saved assumptions, the Fairfield regression reproduces a **$47,575 estimated loss at $460,000 asking**. Switching from a $12,000 bathroom midpoint to $65,000 changes the modeled loss to **$100,575** with other inputs held fixed. These are arithmetic checks against the dated scenario.

- [ ] Record tokens, retries, image failures, cache hits and dollars per successful listing. Add a dry run, a configurable spend ceiling, pause/resume and a rights-cleared 100-listing pilot mode. ### 7. Show how past flips inform a current home

- [ ] Build two distinct panels. **Comparable sales** shows the permitted closed sales actually selected for resale, with sale date, size, distance, price and reason for the match. **Similar past projects** shows permitted verified purchase/resale pairs, hold duration, observed gross spread and evidence for any estimated or documented net result. Surface the matching reasons. With thin or ineligible evidence, explain the gap and suppress unsupported figures.
- [ ] Rank by property type, size, recency, finish and location. Exclude the nearby 662-sq-ft one-bedroom from the Fairfield house comparison. Use the report’s 428 Crofters case to test the evidence labels. Its $150k gross spread, unverified flip status and unknown spend must never become a forecast of $150k profit. Keep raw evidence and reviewer-only notes private.
- [ ] Capture verified costs separately from subjective success/failure labels. Evaluate on later sales, with repeat parcels grouped and inputs limited to those available on the forecast date. Compare today's estimator, an adjusted-comp baseline and history-enhanced estimates on the same cases. Report resale error, range coverage, false profitable calls, and renovation error where invoices exist. Tell buyers what evidence was actually used. Make claims about learning from prior flips only after that method is used and evaluated.
